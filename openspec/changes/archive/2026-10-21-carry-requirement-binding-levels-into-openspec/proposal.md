## Why

The Business Requirement authoring standard (`dev-platform/capabilities/requirement-authoring.md`, section 4) marks every material point with one of three binding levels: **Fixed**, **Meaning fixed, execution variable**, or **Delegated**. The standard governs only the Requirement body. Neither the ADD pipeline (`dev-platform/capabilities/add-intents.md`) nor OpenSpec authoring (`docs/engineering/openspec-workflow.md`, `openspec/config.yaml`) says how those levels carry forward, so the distinction is lost: a point the Requirement wrote as "meaning fixed, presentation up to the executor" can become an exact `SHALL` about one presentation, and an illustrative example or mockup can be read as an exact requirement. The executor then either over-builds against an accidental detail or treats an accepted product meaning as optional.

## What Changes

- OpenSpec authoring guidance (central `docs/engineering/openspec-workflow.md` and the generated `template/docs/engineering/openspec-workflow.md`) gains a section that carries each Requirement point at its own level:
  - **Fixed** decisions are written exactly (verbatim text, order, value, rule) as `SHALL` requirements and scenarios.
  - **Meaning fixed** points are written as the mandatory meaning or properties the user must observe, with the presentation stated as open.
  - **Delegated** details are not raised into requirements; the executor chooses them inside the accepted corridor.
  - A requirement is never bound more tightly than in the source Requirement; examples, illustrative values and mockups do not become exact requirements unless the Requirement fixed them.
  - Financial, technical, security and accessibility constraints and accepted scenarios keep full force even where presentation is delegated.
  - A worked example: a cash-flow report whose balance warning has a fixed meaning (the balance covers only known places, not all of the business's free money, and places without a known balance are flagged next to the balances) and a delegated presentation (banner, caption or icon, wording beyond agreed text, placement inside the strip), next to fixed requirements of the same Requirement (exact switch order, a removed summary card, no horizontal scrolling at 375 px).
- `dev-platform/capabilities/add-intents.md` (and its template mirror): the ADD keeps meaning-fixed and delegated Requirement points as Requirement constraints at their level, and neither an ADD element, an intent nor a handoff envelope hardens them.
- `dev-platform/capabilities/requirement-authoring.md` (and its template mirror): section 4 states that later stages keep these levels and points to the OpenSpec guidance.
- `openspec/config.yaml` and `template/openspec/config.yaml.jinja`: one `specs` rule with the same constraint, so OpenSpec CLI instructions carry it into authoring.
- The rules apply to new authoring; accepted specs are not rewritten.

## Capabilities

### Modified Capabilities

- `openspec-authoring`: OpenSpec authoring preserves Requirement binding levels.

## Impact

- New projects and existing-project updates: managed documentation (`docs/engineering/openspec-workflow.md`, mirrored capability descriptors) and `openspec/config.yaml.jinja`. Copier update of a project that edited `openspec/config.yaml` follows the normal managed-file update path; the change only adds one list entry.
- `template/dev-platform/platform-manifest.json` is regenerated by `scripts/generate_platform_manifest.py --write`, never edited by hand.
- Tests asserting the new rule text in central and generated surfaces.
