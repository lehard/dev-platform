## Context
A job's `providers` list is published once (`publication_queue.publish_job`) and then carried by `pr_review_gate.complete_review`, repair offers and recovery. Job identity is `pr:kind:head:attempt`, and the attempt number doubles as the repair-round budget (`attempts["repair"]`). `work_next` picks the lowest claimable PR. `execute_job` turns any non-zero writer exit into `failed`, which `run_claimed` escalates; escalation drops the job and its providers, so nothing documented leads back.

## Decisions
**One re-offer primitive.** `pr_review_gate.reoffer` appends a single v2 record (one comment, atomic) that returns the candidate to `<kind>-pending` with a new `next_job`. It keeps `task_identity`, `gates`, the findings-bearing `red_gate` and every attempt counter, so the same round is retried and no budget is spent. The job gains an optional `reoffer` object (`seq`, `action`, `from`, `to`, `reason`, `at`) and `job_id` appends `:r<seq>` only when it is present, so existing ids are unchanged and the re-offer is distinct from any completed, claimed or expired job of the same attempt. `seq` comes from a new `attempts["reoffers"]` counter, which survives escalation and so never collides with an earlier result receipt. Provenance is the immutable record itself; nothing is rewritten.

**Two operator commands over that primitive**, on `publication_queue.py`:
- `switch-provider --pr N --provider P [--provider Q ...] --reason TEXT` for `review-pending`, `repair-pending`, `reviewing`/`repairing` whose claim has expired, and `blocked-retryable` review or repair candidates. It refuses while an unexpired claim exists (the claimant would otherwise lose its result silently), for other states, and for an unsupported or duplicate provider. Re-running with the providers already offered is a no-op.
- `resume --pr N --reason TEXT [--provider P ...]` for a `blocked-retryable` review or repair candidate without an automatic job, and for `blocked-escalation` whose gate is an operational repair outcome (`failed`, `rejected`, `no-change`). Findings-level escalations (exhausted rounds, proposed rejection, review rejection, malformed or closed PR) are refused with a message naming the human actions (push a fix, record a disposition, close). Escalation now records the job's providers in its red gate; an older escalation without them needs an explicit `--provider`. The repair red gate is restored from the failed review or required-checks gate so the repair brief again carries the findings.
Both reset the consecutive-unavailable streak of the kind. Re-offered jobs keep the providers list, so later retries, repair and review jobs follow the new provider through the existing carry.

**Worker selection.** `work_next` gains `only_prs` and an `eligible(job)` predicate applied before claiming. `work-next` adds `--pr` (repeatable), `--providers` (review providers this worker offers) and `--repair-provider` (the provider behind `--llm-command`). Each named provider gets one bounded preflight through the existing reviewer readiness probe, run with the same scratch HOME and `--llm-home-file` login files the job would use, so an unusable scratch login is seen before claiming. A review job is eligible when its providers intersect the ready set; a repair job when its providers include a ready repair provider; the review runs on the ordered intersection. Unready providers and their limitations are reported in the result; their jobs are skipped, not claimed. A job whose providers are the unresolved-route sentinel is not eligible for a filtering worker. Without these flags selection is unchanged. `--run` for repair requires `--repair-provider`.

**Unavailable runtime is retryable.** `execute_job` accepts a `runtime_check`; when the writer exits non-zero or cannot start it asks whether the provider runtime is usable (same probe) and reports `unavailable: <limitation>` instead of `failed` when it is not. A start failure is unavailable without a probe. `run_claimed` records, in one transition, `blocked-retryable`, a `repair` gate failed with `{cause: provider-unavailable, providers, limitation}`, the original red gate and `attempts["repair-unavailable"]` incremented; below three consecutive streaks it carries the same-round job with a `retry-unavailable` reoffer, otherwise no job. Review keeps its existing streak and automatic retry, and its red gate gains `cause` and `limitation`. A job result outcome starting with `unavailable` does not complete the job, so an expired claim is reclaimable. Publication treats review- and repair-owned retryable candidates alike and leaves them alone. A writer that ran and failed with a usable runtime still escalates.

**Budget.** A round is the repair job's attempt number. Switch, resume and unavailable retry re-offer the same attempt with a new `reoffers` sequence; only a real review result that produces a new repair offer advances the round.

## Risks and Mitigation
- Preflight cost and drift: one probe per named provider per invocation; a job passing preflight and then failing at launch is classified by the same probe and retried, bounded by the streak.
- Race with a claiming worker: the command refuses under an unexpired claim; a worker that claimed before the switch discards because the observed job no longer equals its job.
- Provider-flag misuse: missing `--repair-provider` and unsupported providers fail with explicit messages; nothing infers a provider.
- Downstream rollout: the new fields are optional and ignored by older readers of the record; only the central source lifecycle runs the coordinator.
